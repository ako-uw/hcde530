## Chart Justifications

**Chart 1 — Bar chart: Top 10 zip codes by coffee license count**
I used a bar chart because I'm comparing counts across discrete categories (zip codes). The result shows that 98104 and 98101 lead by a significant margin, confirming that coffee licensing is concentrated in downtown Seattle.

**Chart 2 — Line chart: New coffee licenses per year**
A line chart is the right choice for showing change over time. The trend line makes the 2025 spike clearly visible and supports the finding that Seattle coffee licensing is still growing rather than plateauing.

**Chart 3 — Heatmap: Zip codes vs years**
A heatmap combines two categorical dimensions into one view, making it easy to spot where and when licensing activity was highest. It answers the third research question in a single visual by showing which neighborhoods became active in which time periods.

**Chart 4 — Horizontal bar chart: NAICS business categories**
A horizontal bar chart works well here because the category names are long and the orientation gives them room. It clearly shows that Snack and Nonalcoholic Beverage Bars dominates, confirming that most Seattle coffee shops are casual venues not formal restaurants.

## C6 — Data Visualization

I built four charts using Plotly Express, each answering a different analytical question from MP1a. Every chart has a title that states the finding rather than just describing the data, labeled axes, and a chart type matched to the data structure: bar for categorical comparison, line for time trend, heatmap for two-dimensional density, and horizontal bar for ranked categories with long labels. Choosing the right chart type for each question was the main judgment call in this assignment.
